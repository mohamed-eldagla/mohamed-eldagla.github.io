Mohamed Eldagla

mohamed.eldagla [at] giu-uni.de

I am currently a pre-doctoral fellow at the Fatima Institute for Global AI Research, working with Alfonso Amayuelas (Stanford HAI) on curiosity-driven learning in LLM agents, a research fellow at PRISM working on reward model interpretability with Rachel Freedman (UC Berkeley CHAI), and a research fellow in the MIT AI Safety Fellowship.

Formally, an MSc candidate in Data Science at the German International University (GIU), where I am also a research and teaching assistant in the Faculty of Informatics, and where I am lucky to be supported by a full fellowship awarded on academic merit. Previously, I completed a Bachelor's degree in Computer Science (Artificial Intelligence and Data Science) at the Egypt-Japan University of Science and Technology (E-JUST), and interned at Université Paris-Est Créteil and the Egyptian Cabinet's Information and Decision Support Center.

My research interests span AI Safety, Interpretability, Foundation Models, NLP, and AI Alignment. So far I have worked on (1) mechanistic interpretability of cross-modal conflict in vision-language models, (2) interpretability of personalized reward models, (3) curiosity-driven learning in LLM agents, and (4) multilingual, multi-turn instruction-following and specification drift evaluation.

I am always open for a conversation, new ideas and projects. If you are interested in any of the topics that I cover or would like to have a chat, feel free to get in touch.
